# Data Analysis Process and Agreement Rate
The data gathered for this study was obtained from the assignments, open class discussion, and individual follow-up interviews. 

We conducted the data analysis of the assignments in pairs: Assignment 1 with Assignment 4, and Assignment 2 with Assignment 3.

## Assignments 1 and 4
Assignment 1 included 12 questions, whereas Assignment 4 had 13. From the total of 25 questions, we selected 15 and conducted open coding. The open coding process was segmented into two phases: 
the preliminary phase and the final phase. In the preliminary phase, three of the authors reviewed the responses of the first five participants and identified a total of 41 unique labels for all 
15 questions. A label refers to a classification associated with a specific  concept that was mentioned by at least one participant in their response to a question, and does not overlap with the 
other labels. For example, in Assignment 4 we asked students to justify their rating regarding the importance of software accessibility. The initial labels identified were: empathy and better 
user-experience. Later, using these labels as the foundation, each author independently reviewed each participant's assignments and categorized their responses into one of the established labels. 
If an author determined none of the  previously defined labels adequately described a participant's response in the assignment under  evaluation, then they proposed a new label. All newly created 
labels and the final outcomes from each author were merged, and later discussed in a meeting attended by the three authors.  During this meeting, the authors examined the entries where there was no 
consensus among all three. The Jaccard index agreement rate for this analysis was 0.626. Referring back to the example question from Assignment 4, after the process concluded, the labels for this 
question were: empathy, better user-experience, fair or moral/ethically correct, legal compliance, and increase user-based. The final number of distinct labels for this pair of assignments amounted 
to 74.

## Assignments 2 and 3
The analysis performed for this pair followed a similar process as that of Assignments 1 and 4: open coding was performed in two phases. During the preliminary phase, the authors identified 9 unique 
labels and 14 categories of issues. The definition of labels in this pair of assignments aligns with the one already provided. Concerning the categories of issues, in these two assignments, students 
were required to identify issues through both manual and automated testing. Based on the issues identified, we grouped them into categories that encompassed issues with similar characteristics. For 
example, MT-004 (i.e., unlabeled UI elements) accounts for missing alt text in images, empty labels, and alike concerns. Given the specificity of the issues mentioned by students in their assignments 
and the fact that multiple success criteria from the WCAG 2.2 guidelines could be applicable to them, we opted to develop our own categories. In the final phase, while the number of labels remained the 
same, the amount of issue categories increased to 29. Following the completion of both phases of the open coding process, one of the authors mapped the categories of issues with the applicable success 
criteria in WCAG 2.2 (i.e., a one-to-many relationship), mapping that was later validated by another author. This analysis revealed two separate agreement rates calculated using the Jaccard index: one 
pertaining to the labels and the other concerning the issues categories, which are 0.421 and 0.520, respectively.
